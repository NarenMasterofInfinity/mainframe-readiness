# From assessment to action

A result is useful when it explains what to do next.

**Example: a scheduler is missing**

- **Observation:** Two jobs refer to an account dataset.
- **Unknown:** Their actual execution order has not been provided.
- **Action:** Obtain the scheduler definition and ask the batch owner to confirm normal and restart order.
- **Possible benefit:** Better evidence for batch confidence and the operations readiness gate.
- **Done when:** The relevant sequence is documented, linked to evidence and reviewed.

The example is illustrative. A recommendation should name its evidence, suggested owner and completion criteria. It should distinguish an evidence-collection action from an architectural option that needs trade-off analysis.

As a stretch feature, compare assessment runs when new artifacts arrive: what was resolved, what remains open and what new findings appeared. More evidence can improve confidence while revealing greater complexity. The objective is an informed planning decision, not a prettier number.
